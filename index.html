What is an Angle?

An angle is formed when two rays meet at a common endpoint called the vertex.

Parts of an Angle

  • Vertex: The common endpoint of the two rays.
  • Arms: The two rays that form the angle.
  • Interior: The region between the two arms.

Measuring Angles

Angles are commonly measured in degrees (°).

One complete revolution = 360°
Half turn = 180°
Quarter turn = 90°

Angle Relationships

Complementary Angles

Two angles are complementary when their sum is 90°.

40° + 50° = 90°

Supplementary Angles

Two angles are supplementary when their sum is 180°.

110° + 70° = 180°

Angles on a Straight Line

Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.

Angles Around a Point

Angles around a point add up to 360°.

Types of Angles

Type Measurement Example
Zero Angle 0° 0°
Acute Angle Greater than 0° and less than 90° 45°
Right Angle Exactly 90° 90°
Obtuse Angle Greater than 90° and less than 180° 120°
Straight Angle Exactly 180° 180°
Reflex Angle Greater than 180° and less than 360° 270°
Complete Angle Exactly 360° 360°

Quick Memory Guide

  • Less than 90° → Acute
  • Exactly 90° → Right
  • Between 90° and 180° → Obtuse
  • Exactly 180° → Straight
  • Between 180° and 360° → Reflex
  • Exactly 360° → Complete
